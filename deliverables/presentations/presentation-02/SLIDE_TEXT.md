Knowledge gap and Literature review

03

OUR KNOWLEDGE GAP

In unfamiliar walking navigation, which information episodes and interactions add attention and interaction burden, which effectively support orientation, action and confirmation, and how do these differences vary with context?

Literature review: Navigation supports arrival, but can still make it supportive or burdensome.

Study

What they tell us

Vaez et al., 2020

Digital navigation changes wayfinding strategies and does not necessarily reduce anxiety.

Wickens, 2008

Concurrent tasks can interfere when they draw on shared processing resources.

Oulasvirta et al., 2005

Urban mobile tasks fragment attention into brief interaction periods.

Cheng et al., 2023

Map information involves a trade-off between spatial learning and cognitive load in VR.

Golab et al., 2022

When pedestrians request instructions varies with environmental and personal factors.

Pielot et al., 2012

Tactile guidance can support eyes-free navigation in everyday use.

Shimokihara et al., 2025

Phone glances correlate with stops and errors; the order of events remains unclear.

Hardwicke et al., 2024

Wayfinding information can help users judge whether a walk is manageable.

Research objectives

07

From navigation episodes to evidence-linked design guidance.

01

Information needs and interaction episodes

Identify information needs and observable interaction episodes across an unfamiliar walking journey.

02

Reported overwhelm and perceived consequences

Identify participant-reported moments of feeling overwhelmed, examine possible sources of difficulty, and explore perceived consequences for enjoyment, confidence, continued exploration and reaching the destination.

03

Classification grounded in context

Develop a classification linking information roles, contextual demands and participant explanations, including contrasting cases.

04

Evidence-linked design guidance

Derive evidence-linked guidance for presenting timing, detail and delivery, with clear limits on what has been evaluated.

Why start with six participants?

10

A time-bounded, exploratory study

Each case combines a walking observation with an in-depth interview.
Six is our initial target for a focused study, with limited generalisability.

01

Purposeful selection before the study

Select participants who meet our study criteria, so each case is highly relevant
to unfamiliar walking navigation among newcomers to Hong Kong.

02

Feasible scope, with room for depth

Six cases fit the project schedule and allow detailed observation,
interviewing, within-case analysis and cross-case comparison.

03

Iterative recruitment towards analytical sufficiency

If six cases provide insufficient evidence, recruitment will continue until interpretations are
adequately supported or additional cases yield little substantive new insight.

## Frame 17

[Open source frame](https://www.figma.com/design/ul5mPWNMdrkycxnLEV9Owh/5008-Research?node-id=331-10)

Observable action codebook

22

Seven initial action labels; co-coding is allowed. Causes and burden remain separate.

Code

Observable evidence

Boundary or exclusion

STOP

A visible halt in walking

Includes traffic, rest or photos. Context is required.

PHONE_VIEW

Discernible gaze at the phone

Holding a phone alone is insufficient.

PHONE_INPUT

Visible phone tap, swipe or other input

Do not infer which app or content without evidence.

HEAD_TURN

Visible head-direction change

Does not itself establish sign reading.

SIGN_VIEW

Evidence of viewing an identifiable sign

Use uncertain if the target cannot be established.

HELP_REQUEST

Audible or reliably noted request for assistance

Ordinary talk or ambiguous gestures are insufficient.

R_PROMPT

Researcher instruction or intervention

Log timing, content and reason.

Group operational definitions. Coding principles: Wolff et al. (2024); Felner & Henderson (2022). Pilot calibration remains TBC.

New codes may be added as behaviours emerge in interviews, e.g. PHONE_SEARCH
for searching for navigation-related information on a phone.
